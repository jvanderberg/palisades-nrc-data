Dear Mike Mlynarek:

On November 30, 2023, the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) completed onsite
inspection activities for October 10, 2023, through November 30, 2023, at the permanently
shut-down Palisades Nuclear Plant in Covert, Michigan. The purpose of the inspection was to
determine whether decommissioning activities were conducted safely and in accordance with
NRC requirements. The enclosed report presents the results of this inspection, which were
discussed with you and other members of your staff on January 23, 2024.

During the inspection period, the NRC inspectors reviewed the following aspects of onsite
activities: problem identification and resolution; fire protection; and decommissioning
performance. The inspection consisted of an examination of activities at the site as they relate
to safety and compliance with the Commission’s rules and regulations. Areas examined during
the inspection are identified in the enclosed report. Within these areas, the inspection consisted
of a selective examination of procedures and representative records, reviewing work activities,
and interviews with personnel.

Based on the results of this inspection, the NRC identified one Severity Level (SL) IV violation of
NRC requirements. However, because of the very low safety significance and because the
issue was entered into your corrective action program (CAP), the NRC is treating the issue as a
Non-Cited Violation (NCV), in accordance with Section 2.3.2 of the NRC's Enforcement Policy.

No response is required for the NCV. However, if you contest the violation or significance of the
NCV, you should provide a response within 30 days of the date of this inspection report, with the
basis for your denial, to the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission, ATTN: Document Control
Desk, Washington, DC 20555–0001, with copies to the Regional Administrator, Region III; and
the Director, Office of Enforcement.

                                     EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

                                     Palisades Nuclear Plant

                    NRC Inspection Report Nos. 05000255/2023004(DRSS)

The Palisades Nuclear Plant is permanently shut-down and defueled power reactor. This
periodic safety inspection reviewed licensed activities associated with decommissioning
performance; occupational radiation exposure; radiological surveys; and radioactive waste
treatment, effluent, and environmental monitoring.

Problem Identification and Resolution

•   Issues were identified by the licensee at appropriate thresholds and entered into the
    corrective action program (CAP). Issues were screened and prioritized commensurate with
    safety significance. Licensee evaluations determined the significance of issues and included
    appropriate remedial corrective actions.

Fire Protection Program

•   An effective decommissioning Fire Protection Program was maintained and implemented
    that reasonably prevented fires; provided the capability to rapidly detect, control, and
    extinguish fires that could result in radiological hazards; and ensured the risk of fire-induced
    hazards to the public, environment, and plant personnel were minimized.

Decommissioning Performance and Status Review

•   The NRC identified one SL IV NCV of 10 CFR 50.82(a)(8)(i) for using the Palisades
    Decommissioning Trust Fund (DTF) for expenses that were not legitimate decommissioning
    activities.

Summary of Plant Activities

During this inspection period, the licensee continued activities associated with Period 2 of the
Post Shutdown Decommissioning Activities Report (PSDAR). No major decommissioning
activities occurred during the inspection period.

1.0    Problem Identification and Resolution at Permanently Shutdown Reactors
       (IP 40801)

1.1    Inspection Scope

       The inspectors performed walkdowns, reviewed documents, and interviewed plant
       personnel to assess the licensee’s performance in the following areas:

       •   Effectiveness at preventing, detecting, and correcting issues;
       •   Identifying and evaluating potential 10 CFR Part 21, “Reporting of Defects and
           Non-Compliance Issues;”
       •   Audits and assessments evaluating the Corrective Action Program and Quality
           Assurance Program; and
       •   The licensee’s safety culture


1.2    Observations and Findings

       The inspectors observed several corrective action program (CAP) management review
       meetings to assess the manner in which management is performing oversight of the
       CAP. The inspectors reviewed various issues entered into the CAP to assess whether
       issues were being assigned the appropriate significance level, investigation level, and
       corrective actions. There were no root cause evaluations or apparent cause evaluations
       conducted during the inspection period; however, the inspectors reviewed various work
       group evaluations. Corrective actions were also reviewed to verify that the assigned
       corrective actions were completed as described in the CAP documents. Through
       document review and discussions with staff, the inspectors evaluated the licensee’s
       process for reviewing 10 CFR Part 21 issues as well as completing audits in accordance
       with the quality assurance program manual. The inspectors reviewed the most recent
       audits of the CAP and Quality Assurance Program. The inspectors also evaluated the
       licensee’s safety culture through review of programs such as the employee concerns
       program, safety culture surveys conducted by the licensee, and discussions with site
       staff.

       No findings were identified.

1.3    Conclusions

       Issues were identified by the licensee at appropriate thresholds and entered into the
       CAP. Issues were screened and prioritized commensurate with safety significance.
       Licensee evaluations determined the significance of issues and included appropriate
       remedial corrective actions.

2.0   Fire Protection Program at Permanently Shutdown Reactors (IP 64704)

2.1   Inspection Scope

      The inspectors performed walkdowns, reviewed documents, and interviewed plant
      personnel to assess the licensee’s performance in the following areas:

      •   Fire protection program met Technical Specifications, PSDAR, and fire hazard
          analyses requirements;
      •   Changes to the Fire Protection Program did not reduce the effectiveness of the
          program;
      •   Assessments were being performed in accordance with 10 CFR 50.48(f)(2);
      •   Fire protection detection and suppression systems were effectively maintained,
          surveillances were performed, and systems were capable of performing their
          intended function;
      •   Fire barriers were effectively maintained;
      •   Firefighting equipment was properly inventoried, inspected, tested, and maintained;
      •   Administrative controls were in place to minimize the occurrence of a fire; and
      •   Staffing and training requirements were consistent with the Fire Protection Program
          and Emergency Plan.

      The inspectors verified that when issues were identified, licensee personnel
      appropriately documented the issue in the CAP.

2.2   Observations and Findings

      The inspectors walked down the fuel handling building and east radwaste building to
      verify that fire protection equipment aligned with programmatic requirements and
      reviewed surveillance records for fire detection and suppression equipment in these
      areas. The inspectors reviewed current fire brigade training and drill participation records
      for each crew. Evaluations for modifications to the fire protection program for the
      feedwater purity building and cooling towers were reviewed to verify that the changes did
      not reduce the effectiveness of the fire protection program. The most recent internal
      audits of the fire protection program were reviewed to assess the licensee’s ability to
      self-identify issues.

      No findings were identified.

2.3   Conclusions

      An effective decommissioning Fire Protection Program was maintained and
      implemented that reasonably prevented fires; provided the capability to rapidly detect,
      control, and extinguish fires that could result in radiological hazards; and ensured the
      risk of fire-induced hazards to the public, environment, and plant personnel were
      minimized.

3.0   Decommissioning Performance and Status Review (IP 71801)

3.1   Inspection Scope

      The inspectors reviewed documents and interviewed plant personnel to assess the
      licensee’s performance in the following areas:

      •   Withdrawals from the Decommissioning Trust Fund (DTF) were consistent with the
          requirements established in 10 CFR 50.82(a)(8).

      The inspectors verified that when issues were identified, licensee personnel
      appropriately documented the issue in the CAP.

3.2   Observations and Findings

      In inspection report 05000255/2023003 (ML23276B452), an unresolved item was
      identified involving withdrawals from the DTF in the amount of approximately $53,500 for
      seven items identified as restart projects. This item was being tracked by NRC to
      determine if those withdrawals were used for legitimate decommissioning activities and
      allowable under NRC regulations (URI 05000255/2023003-01, Review of
      Decommissioning Trust Fund Withdrawals). As the NRC continued its review of
      withdrawals from the DTF, the inspectors identified two instances in which the DTF was
      used for voluntary contributions to community events/support activities, totaling
      approximately $3,500.

      With respect to the above restart expenditures, the licensee had a system in place to
      control the source of payments but had inadvertently used the DTF due to mis-coding
      errors. The licensee promptly arranged for replacement of those funds in the DTF and
      performed additional training of staff. The improper use of the DTF for voluntary
      contributions to community events/support activities reflected non-conservative decision
      making with respect to the DTF. Once brought to its attention during the NRC inspection,
      the licensee re-evaluated and arranged for replacement of those funds in the DTF.

      Violation

      The NRC identified one Severity Level IV non-cited violation (NCV) of 10 CFR
      50.82(a)(8)(i) because HDI used DTF for expenses that were not legitimate
      decommissioning activities. Specifically, HDI used funds for restart activities and
      voluntary contributions to community events/support activities that were not related to
      removing the facility or site safely from service and reducing residual activity to a level
      that permits release of the property for either unrestricted or restricted conditions and
      termination of the license. This violation closes out URI 05000255/2023003-01, Review
      of Decommissioning Trust Fund Withdrawals.

      10 CFR 50.82(a)(8)(i) states, in part, that decommissioning trust funds may be used by
      licensees if the withdrawals are for expenses for legitimate decommissioning activities
      consistent with the definition of decommissioning in 10 CFR 50.2.




                                               5
      10 CFR 50.2 defines decommissioning as removing a facility or site safely from service
      and reducing residual radioactivity to a level that permits—
             (1) Release of the property for unrestricted use and termination of the license; or
             (2) Release of the property under restricted conditions and termination of the
                 license.

      Contrary to the above, between January 2023 and March 2023, HDI used the Palisades
      DTF for expenses that were not legitimate decommissioning activities. Specifically, HDI
      used decommissioning funds for some activities identified as being restart activities; as
      well as voluntary contributions to community events/support activities. These
      expenditures were not related to removing the facility or site safely from service, and
      reducing residual activity to a level that permits release of the property for either
      unrestricted or restricted conditions and termination of the license.

      This violation was determined to be a Severity Level IV violation. The NRC considered
      NRC Enforcement Policy example 6.3.c.1, dated January 14, 2022, regarding a
      significant failure to meet decommissioning as required by regulation or license condition
      for materials sites. Additionally, the NRC considered the legitimacy of use, the dollar
      amount involved compared to the total DTF amount, and programmatic aspects in
      making this determination. The NRC determined that the DTF was illegitimately used,
      $57,000 is a small fraction of the Palisades DTF and did not represent a significant
      failure to meet decommissioning, and that this was not a single failure event, there were
      numerous occurrences. HDI entered this issue into the CAP as PAL-10248 and
      PAL-01605 and initiated corrective actions to restore the funds back into the DTF.

      Since the licensee placed the deficiency into its CAP, the violation was of very low safety
      significance, and because the violation was not willful or repetitive, this violation is being
      treated as an NCV, consistent with Section 2.3.2.a of the NRC Enforcement Policy.
      (NCV 05000255/2023004-01, Improper Use of Decommissioning Trust Fund).

3.3   Conclusions

      One Severity Level IV NCV of 10 CFR 50.82(a)(8)(i) was identified because HDI used
      the DTF for expenses that were not legitimate decommissioning activities.

4.0   Exit Meeting

      The inspectors presented the results of the inspection to Mike Mlynarek and other
      members of the Palisades Nuclear Plant staff at an exit meeting on January 23, 2024.
      The licensee acknowledged the results presented and did not identify any of the
      information discussed as proprietary.
