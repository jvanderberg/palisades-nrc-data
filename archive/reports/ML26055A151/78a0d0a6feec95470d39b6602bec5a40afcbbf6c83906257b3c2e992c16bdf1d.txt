INSPECTION RESULTS

 Minor Violation                                                                   71111.21N.05
 Minor Violation: Palisades had the option of hydrostatically pressure testing fire hoses
 every three years or replacing them with new fire hoses every five years. Work Order
 (WO) 53007241-01 was initiated to replace all fire hoses prior to the site entering technical
 specifications on August 26, 2025. Due to site procurement delays, all fire hoses were not
 replaced or hydrostatically pressure tested as required with some fire hoses last replaced
 in 2018.

  Prior to this inspection, condition report (CR) 25014235, “Fire Hose Replacement or
  Hydrostatic Test not Complete,” was initiated on November 11, 2025. This CR identified
  scheduling conflicts prevented hydrostatic pressure testing of fire hoses which were not
  replaced. At this time, the site failed to establish compensatory actions for the affected fire
  hoses as required per Procedure FPIP-4, “Fire Protection Systems and Fire Protection
  Equipment,” Revision 45.

  The inspectors questioned the number and installed location of the expired fire hoses.
  In response to the inspectors’ question, the site identified 30 fire hoses which failed to be
  tested or replaced at the required frequency including some located inside the power block.




                                                  3
Screening: The inspectors determined that the performance deficiency for the failure to test or
replace fire hoses at the required frequency was a minor since the fire brigade were trained to
bring a high-rise fire hose when responding to fire events. The installed expired fire hoses
would still perform their function at some capacity, and due to the current defueled state of
the plant there was no potential release from the core in event of fire.

Enforcement: License Condition 2.C.(3) stated that Palisades Energy shall implement and
maintain in effect all provisions of the approved fire protection program. Site Procedure
FPIP-4, “Fire Protection Systems and Fire Protection Equipment,” Revision 45 was the
implementing procedure of the approved fire protection program which required licensee to
establish compensatory actions for the affected fire hoses.

As a result of this inspection, all power block fire hoses were replaced upon discovery and
compensatory measure, including fire tours, in accordance with FPIP-4 were established
for the remaining fire hose locations. CR 25015303 “Fire Hose to be Hydrostatic Tested
or Replaced,” was initiated on December 17, 2025, documenting this issue.

This failure to comply with requirement of FPIP-4 and establish compensatory actions
constitutes a minor violation that is not subject to enforcement action in accordance with
the NRC’s Enforcement Policy.

Minor Violation                                                                 71111.21N.05
Minor Violation: During a walkdown of the Auxiliary Feedwater Pump P-8D enclosure, the
inspectors questioned the presence of combustibles in the 5-foot perimeter. Combustibles
included a temporary metal office with windows within 3 feet of the east side of the P-8D
enclosure/trailer and additional combustibles laying on the ground.

Attachment 1 of Procedure ENG-329, “Control of Combustibles,” identified Fire Area 59,
the auxiliary feedwater Pump P-8D enclosure/trailer, as Level 1 combustible area including
a 5-foot perimeter surrounding this fire area. Compensatory measures were required for any
transient combustibles in the area.

Screening: The inspectors determined that the failure to maintain Level 1 combustible
controls for P-8D was a minor performance deficiency since pump P-8D was not required to
be functional for the current plant shutdown conditions.

Enforcement: License Condition 2.C.(3) stated Palisades Energy shall implement and
maintain in effect all provisions of the approved fire protection program. Site Procedure
ENG-329, “Control of Combustibles,” Revision 2 was the implementing procedure of the
approved fire protection program. Attachment 1 of ENG-329 identified Fire Area 59,
Pump P-8D as a Level 1 combustible area including a 5-foot perimeter surrounding this
fire area.

Contrary to this requirement, as of December 9, 2025, transient combustibles were found
within 3 feet of P-8D trailer, and no compensatory measures were established.

In response to the inspectors’ concern, the site-initiated CR 25015052 “Combustible Materials
were Found Contrary to ENG-329.” The site removed the combustible materials and planned
to remove the office building prior to declaring the P-8D system functional.

Minor Violation                                                                  71111.21N.05

Minor Violation: The inspectors requested the previous work orders documentation of the
two diesel driven fire water pump (P-9B and P-41,) engine (K-5 and K-10) inspection, and
preventive maintenance which was completed during the shutdown period. The site failed
to locate three work orders in records including WO 00584081-01 for Pump P-9B, 2023
WO 50083624-01 for P-41, and 2025 WO 52990823-01 for P-41. Upon further searching,
two of the three missing WOs were located outside of the document control and records
management process. The 2023 WO 50083624-01 for P-41/K-10 engine inspection was not
located by the exit for this inspection.

Screening: The inspectors determined that the performance deficiency was minor since the
most recent completed work order in 2025 for P-41/K-10 was available and provided
assurance that the engine was successfully inspected.

Enforcement: License Condition 2.C.(3) stated Palisades Energy shall implement and
maintain in effect all provisions of the approved fire protection program. Site Procedure
FPIP-4, “Fire Protection Systems and Fire Protection Equipment,” Section 20.2 was the
implementing procedure of the approved fire protection program and required records to
be filed in accordance with the document control and records management process.

Procedure DSP-RM-001, “Document Control and Records Management Process,”
which required official records to be transmitted within 30 days after record completion.

Contrary to the above requirements, the licensee failed to maintain the official records for
WO 50083624-01 for P-41/K-10 fire engine inspection. The licensee entered this issue into
their corrective action program and initiated CR 25015056 “Failure to meet Procedure
DSP-RM-001 Requirement for Transmitting Official Records.”

This failure to comply with DSP-RM-001 constitutes a minor violation that is not subject
to enforcement action in accordance with the NRC’s Enforcement Policy.

Minor Violation                                                              71111.21N.05
Minor Violation: During performance of Procedure FPSP-RO-10, “Fire Water Suppression
System Flow Test,” on May 08, 2023, per WO 52965257-01, unexpected results were
encountered during Section 5.4 and 5.5 for the inside loop path 2 and 3 when compared to
the previous 2017 flow performance test. Path 2 was the branch connection to the turbine
building fire hoses through valve MV-FP283 and MV-FP1162. Path 3 was the branch
connection to the transformer deluge through valves MV-FP282 and MV-FP117.

The flow tests for these two paths have only been performed twice since they were
added to the procedure in 2016. In the 2017 tests, the inside loop path 2 flow was
1099 gallon-per-minute (gpm) and the path 3 flow was 1135 gpm. In the 2023 tests,
the inside loop path 2 flow was 832 gpm and the path 3 flow was 774 gpm. CR PAL-01124
“FPSP-RO-10 Fire Suppression Water System Flow Test Degraded,” was initiated at that
time and noted there maybe flow restrictions in the fire system cross tie or associated piping.


                                              5
The CR was closed to work request (WR) 01124 to inspect MV-FP283 internals to check
for obstruction.

During the inspectors’ review of Procedure FPSP-RO-10, the inspectors noticed that the
procedure did not include acceptance criteria for testing the fire water suppression flow for
these two paths.

The site noted that WO 00589203 for WR 01124 was required to be completed during the
fire protection system return-to-service plan or a justification would be needed.

Screening: The inspectors determined that the performance deficiency for failure to include
acceptance criteria in Procedure FPSP-RO-10 for testing inside loop paths 2 and 3 was minor
because the unacceptable results for paths 2 and 3 were recognized by the licensee and
documented in CR PAL-01124.

Enforcement: License Condition 2.C.(3) stated that Palisades Energy shall implement
and maintain in effect all previsions of the approved fire protection program. Procedure
FPSP-RO-10 was the implementing procedure for testing the fire water suppression system
flow.

Contrary to the above, Procedure FPSP-RO-10 failed to include the acceptance criteria
for the inside loop paths 2 and 3. The licensee documented this issue in CR 25015259
“FPSP-RO-10 did not include Acceptance Criteria for Flow Test.”

This failure to comply with the above requirements constitutes a minor violation that is
not subject to enforcement action in accordance with the NRC’s Enforcement Policy.

Observation: Vast Majority of PMs for AFW P-8D have No Work Orders              71111.21N.05
Auxiliary feedwater (AFW) pump, P-8D, was installed under Engineering Change (EC) 55441
for Palisade’s transition to National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) Standard 805 to
address fire scenarios that may result in a loss of feedwater to the steam generators.
EC 55441 installed P-8D as a diverse, manually operated AFW pump such that the train
would be free of fire damage and other scenarios that may impact the operation of the primary
AFW system trains, P-8A, 8B and 8C. This modification was credited for probabilistic risk
assessment (PRA) risk reduction. The EC closeout was completed on November 11, 2019.

During this inspection, the inspectors walked down the P-8D system and questioned what
preventive maintenance (PM) had been performed since installation and what PMs were
completed to ensure the system would function when required for plant start up. The site
provided an excel spreadsheet which listed 102 PMs related to maintenance of P-8D with
92 PMs never performed. Additionally, 72 PMs were either not scheduled or did not have a
WO generated for performance. The site also noted, no model WOs were drafted for a vast
majority of the PMs. The site planned to screen the 102 PMs to identify which ones were
necessary for operation of P-8D. In addition, at the time of this inspection, the water source
for the P-8D system, the demineralized water tank, T-939, was out of service. The site noted
EC 93368 planned to restore T-939 to service prior to restart and noted P-8D cannot be tested
until T-939 was restored and filled with water.

The site stated the P-8D system would be considered non-functional if the PMs and testing
were not complete prior to restart. Compensatory actions would also need to be re-instituted


                                              6
including performance of fire tours in Fire Area 2, cable spreading room, and Fire Area 4,
1-C switchgear room. The site noted P-8D was very important for the fire and flood PRAs
with the unavailable, relative quantitative core damage risk increasing for fire by a factor
(RAW score) of 4.7 and for flood by 5.3 in the updated 2025 PRA models. This is being
documented as an observation, and no associated performance deficiency was identified at
the time of the inspection since P-8D is not required to be functional during extended plant
shutdown conditions.

Observation: Fire Door 44 Impairment for Bottom Minimum Gap                      71111.21N.05
Not Scheduled for Repair
Condition report PAL-01445 “Door 44 Bottom Gap did not Meet Minimum Requirement,”
was initiated in October 2023, identified a deficiency with Fire Door 44 for the cable spreading
room double door exit in Fire Area 2/Room 224. The site identified Fire Door 44 does not meet
the minimum gap acceptance criteria as specified in Procedure FPSP-SO-2, “Inspection and
Testing of Palisades Plant Fire Doors,” Revision 14. The acceptance criteria required Door
44’s bottom gap to be between 5/8 to 1 inch. However, there were areas where the door gap
were smaller than 5/8 inch. Work Order 00588199 was initiated to resolve this discrepancy
and has not been planned or completed. Compensatory actions for Fire Door 44 were in place
at the time of the inspection, and it was tracked on the fire impairment list. The inspectors
determined that the failure to ensure that Door-44 bottom gap met the acceptance criteria
as specified in FPSP-SO-2 was a minor performance deficiency. Additionally, this observation
documents incomplete corrective actions for a performance deficiency that was over
2 years old with WO 00588199 not in scope for work to be complete prior to plant restart.
The licensee documented this observation in CR 26010704 “Door-44 Not Meeting Acceptance
Criteria was Not Completed in Timely Manner.”

Observation: Failure to Ground a Scaffold Installed Near Energized High          71111.21N.05
Energy Equipment
During a walkdown of Fire Area 2/Room 224, cable spreading room, the inspectors noted the
amount of scaffolding installed to support installation of engineering changes for the site’s
transition to NFPA 805. The inspectors reviewed the scaffolding installation form completed
on January 29, 2025, and questioned if the scaffolding forms were updated since electrical
equipment was re-energized when the site transitioned to operating technical specifications
on August 26, 2025. Specifically, during walkdowns the inspectors noted scaffolding near
transformers EX-11, EX-12, and Load Centers EB-11 and EB-12. Procedure MA-018,
“Control of Scaffolding,” Revision 1, Step 7.1.5 required that for scaffold near energized
high energy (>600 volts) equipment, the first piece of installed scaffold shall be grounded
by use of ground straps or cables to reduce the risk of electric shock.

In response to inspectors’ question, the site determined that the scaffolding was required
to be grounded and initiated CR 25015517 “Scaffolding Installed in Close Proximity to
Transformers,” on December 23, 2025, to document this issue. Additionally, the site performed
a review of all scaffolding required for the NFPA 805 projects and identified where grounding
was required. The inspectors determined this performance deficiency was minor since there
was no effect on plant equipment.
