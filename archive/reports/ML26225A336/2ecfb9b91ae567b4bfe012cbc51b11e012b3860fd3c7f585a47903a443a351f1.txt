 Licensee-Identified Non-Cited Violation                                         71111.20
 This violation of very low safety significance was identified by the licensee and has been
 entered into the licensee corrective action program and is being treated as a non-cited
 violation, consistent with Section 2.3.2 of the Enforcement Policy.
 Violation:

 Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) Part 26, “Fitness for Duty Programs,”
 Subpart 205, “Work Hours,” requires, in part, that licensees shall control work hours so an
 individual’s hours worked do not exceed 16 hours in any 24-hour period, 26 hours in any
 48-hour period, and 72 hours in any 7-day period. Additionally, it requires, in part, that
 licensees shall ensure that individuals have, at a minimum, a 34-hour break in any 9-day
 period.

 Contrary to the above, between March 28, 2026, and April 3, 2026, the licensee failed to
 ensure that individuals subject to the requirements of 10 CFR Part 26 complied with the
 work hour limitations and scheduling restrictions of Part 26. Specifically, the licensee failed
 to adequately schedule and control the work hours of 3 individuals performing work
 covered by the restrictions of 10 CFR Part 26 by not ensuring that the individuals ’ work
 hours did not exceed 72 hours in any 7-day period. Additionally, the licensee failed to
 ensure that the same individuals received at least a 34-hour break in any 9-day period.

 Significance/Severity: Green. The inspectors assessed the significance of the finding
 using IMC 0609 Appendix A, “The Significance Determination Process (SDP) for Findings
 At-Power.” Using Exhibit 2, “Mitigating Systems Screening Questions,” of IMC 0609
 Appendix A, Section A, the inspectors answered “No” to all of the questions, screening the
 finding to Green, as the work completed by the three individuals was verified by individuals
 who did meet the requirements of the Part 26 work hours restrictions, ensuring that the
 equipment maintained either its operability or functionality.

Assessment                                                                           71152A
RP Waste Shipment Exceeded DOT Limits

On September 23, 2025, the licensee transported radioactive waste off-site as exclusive use
shipment 2025-RW-017. On September 24, 2025, the Radiation Protection (RP) Shipping
Specialist recognized that the shipment was sent with dose rates exceeding Department of
Transportation (DOT) regulations in 49 CFR 173.441. When recognized, the licensee had the
truck return to the site, which arrived on September 25, 2026. This issue was reviewed by the
inspectors, and a Green Licensee-Identified non-cited violation was documented in Inspection
Report 05000255/2025005, Section 71124.08.

The licensee also performed a root cause evaluation for this issue. The inspectors reviewed
the root cause evaluation, corrective actions taken and planned, and effectiveness reviews of
corrective actions completed and planned. The licensee identified the root cause to be
associated with the radiological shipping process which lacked clear guidance where it
allowed the same individuals that performed required surveys and the individuals who
prepared the shipping paperwork to also review and approve the shipment documentation
without independent verification of administrative dose limits. This gap created conditions
where human error went undetected. The corrective action to preclude repetition was to
revise licensee procedure RP-412, Radioactive Shipping, to require an independent review of
the shipping checklist to ensure compliance with all applicable requirements.

The inspectors independently reviewed the causal analysis process, corrective actions
(including revisions to RP-412), shipments completed after corrective actions were
implemented, and effectiveness reviews of corrective actions completed. During the review of
corrective actions, the inspectors questioned the due date extension of a corrective action to
preclude repetition without Management Review Committee (MRC) Chair approval, which is
contrary to PI-001, Corrective Action Program, section 7.5.3.c.4. It was also determined that
four additional corrective actions had their due dates extended without a basis for why the
extension is acceptable, necessary, and the risk of extending the action in the request. This
issue was documented in the corrective action program as CR 25015623, NRC ID: Improper
Extension of Adverse CR Actions. The inspectors determined that this was a minor
performance deficiency of PI-001.

No violations were identified.

The inspectors identified a Green finding and associated non-cited violation (NCV) of
Technical Specification Section 5.4.1.a when the licensee failed to provide work instructions
appropriate for the work activities. Specifically, in the first example, Work Order
(WO) 588976-09 lacked sufficient detail for work near buried utilities. Second, troubleshooting
activities were performed on the failed cables without written work instructions.



                                              10
Description:

On February 5, 2026, horizontal directional drilling (HDD) was being performed on site in
support of the fiber backbone project. The HDD was being performed by three different
vendors supporting the work. One vendor performed overall job oversight, another vendor
performed the HDD, and a third vendor performed ground-penetrating radar (GPR) activities
to support the HDD.

Due to recent snowfall, the GPR was unable to be effectively performed and the vendor
technician utilized a less reliable radio detector in order to support identification of any
underground interferences. The radio detector did not identify underground interferences,
cables, in the areas scanned. The HDD vendor proceeded with HDD and during the pullback,
the reamer attached to three previously unidentified underground cables, severing two and
stretching one. At 13:07, the site lost power to the switchyard rear bus and class 1E
safeguards bus. Emergency Diesel Generator (EDG) 1-2 automatically started, as expected,
due to the loss of the safeguards bus, to power in-plant class 1E bus 1D. Work was
immediately halted.

Subsequently, during troubleshooting activities to identify the underground cables impacted,
EDG 1-2 unexpectedly cycled three times. The cause of the cycling was unknown to
operators and EDG 1-2 was secured. It was determined that a signal transmitting device had
been connected to the damaged cables in order to locate the cable electrical flow-path using
a receiver. The device emitted a 50 VDC/50 Hz signal that was introduced into plant control
circuitry and caused the cycling of EDG 1-2.

The licensee performed two apparent cause evaluations (ACEs) for this event. In both ACEs,
the licensee identified issues with work instructions or troubleshooting governance and
insufficient field oversight during the work activity that resulted in 1) the damage to three
previously unidentified underground cables during HDD that caused the loss of power to the
switchyard rear bus and class 1E safeguards bus resulting in the automatic actuation of EDG
1-2, and 2) the cycling of EDG 1-2 due to a signal transmitting device that was introduced into
the plant control circuitry during unauthorized troubleshooting of the damaged cables.

This issue was also reviewed using Inspection Procedure 71153, “Follow up of Events and
Notices of Enforcement Discretion,” and was reported in Licensee Event Report (LER)
05000255/2026-001-00, “Human Performance Error Resulted in Emergency Diesel Generator
(EDG) 1-2 Actuation” (ADAMS Accession No. ML26124A222).

Corrective Actions: The licensee entered AOP-9, Loss of D Bus, for this issue and all drilling
activities were stopped. The licensee completed two ACEs to investigate the causes for the
damage to the cables and unapproved troubleshooting activities. They subsequently
performed repair activities to the damaged cables after troubleshooting was completed. In
addition, the site developed a return to work plan for additional activities associated with the
fiber backbone project.

Corrective Action References: CR 26011187 and CR 26011372
Performance Assessment:

Performance Deficiency: The licensee had two examples of failure to provide work
instructions appropriate for the work activities for the same event. In the first example, the


                                               11
licensee failed to ensure information generated in the engineering change and advanced work
authorization were incorporated into the work order task used for directional drilling activities
and to ensure the work instructions had sufficient detail for work in the area where there were
known buried utilities. Specifically, the work order task for HDD did not provide appropriate
detail for personnel performing the tasks to understand the risk commensurate with the work
being performed. Second, the licensee failed to ensure appropriate work control or
troubleshooting governance was provided to perform troubleshooting activities to identify the
underground cables impacted during HDD activities, causing EDG 1-2 to unexpectedly cycle
during operation.

Screening and Significance: The inspectors assessed the significance of the finding
using IMC 0609 Appendix A, “The Significance Determination Process (SDP) for Findings
At-Power.” The inspectors used IMC 0609.04, “Initial Characterization of Findings,” and
IMC 0609 Appendix A, Exhibit 2, “Mitigating Systems Screening Questions.” The finding was
determined to be of very low safety significance (Green) after questions for “Mitigating SSCs
and PRA Functionality” were answered as “No.”

Cross-Cutting Area: Human Performance The licensee did not ensure that the work process
included the identification and management of risk commensurate to the work and the need
for coordination with different groups. Specifically, in the first example, the licensee did not
ensure that risk was managed commensurate with the work when the work package lacked
sufficient detail and clear directions for work near buried utilities. Second, the vendor team
perceived permission to move forward with troubleshooting without written work instructions
and had an inadequate risk perception of the troubleshooting activity.
Enforcement:

Violation: Technical Specification Section 5.4.1.a requires, in part, that written procedures
shall be established, implemented, and maintained covering the applicable procedures
recommended in Regulatory Guide 1.33, Revision 2, Appendix A, February 1978.
NRC Regulatory Guide 1.33, Revision 2, Appendix A, Section 9 addresses “Procedures for
Performing Maintenance,” and Section 9.a states, in part, that maintenance that can affect the
performance of safety-related equipment should be properly pre-planned and performed in
accordance with written procedures, documented instructions, or drawings appropriate to the
circumstances.

Contrary to the above, on February 5, 2026, the licensee failed to provide work instructions
appropriate for required work activities. Specifically, in the first example, WO 588976-09
lacked sufficient detail for work near buried utilities. Second, troubleshooting activities were
performed on the failed cables without written work instructions.

Enforcement Action: This violation is being treated as a non-cited violation, consistent with

Observation: Extent of Condition for Water Intrusion in Instrument Air System        71152A
On March 13, 2026, the licensee initiated CR 26012619, CV-3025 Not Operating as Expected,
documenting that water was found in the air line of the shutdown cooling heat exchanger E-
60A/B outlet valve, CV-3025. As part of the corrective actions, the licensee performed
additional investigation and found water in the upstream air filter and drain lines of CV-3025.
The licensee then identified that the instrument air system (IAS) temporary modification dryer
status indicator lights and panel had no power and were not in service. They generated CR
26012629, Temporary Instrument Air Dryer Lost Power, and CR 26012630, Temporary IAS
Compressor Dryer Lost Power. The licensee noted it was unknown how long the dryer was out
of service.

One of the suggested actions for CR 26012629 was to perform an extent of condition
review; however, the CR completion notes stated the extent of condition was addressed by
CR 26012619. The inspectors reviewed CR 26012619 and did not identify any extent of
condition actions that addressed potential water intrusion into the IAS as a whole, as the
actions in CR 26012619 were focused specifically on CV-3025.

The inspectors reviewed the CRs relating to the water intrusion, site procedures, and the
failure mode analysis for the event. The inspectors then followed up with the licensee to
inquire about extent of condition for all of the IAS. The actions included selecting a sample of
the system’s valves and evaluating those for potential water intrusion. Also, system venting
and draining was performed to remove excess water. However, the inspectors identified that
the valves selected were focused on low points in the system and were related to the ongoing
passivation activity at the time. During discussions between the inspectors and the licensee
staff, another CR was generated, CR 26015169, Air Operated Valves Identified for Inspection
of Moisture Intrusion in Air Lines, which recommended internal inspections of air regulators on
safety-related IAS valves to look for water/moisture. This CR provided additional scope
beyond the previous CR’s more focused actions.

The inspectors identified a lack of conservative bias in the actions taken during the original
extent of condition review in CR 26012619. The licensee addressed this concern in
CR 26015169, which focused on instrument air as a whole system, whereas CR 26012619
was focused only on CV-3025. No findings or violations of significance were identified during
this review.

Observation: Rear and Safeguards Bus Lost when Control Cables were Damaged 71152A
On February 5, 2026, the licensee was performing horizontal directional drilling (HDD) on-site
when the reamer attached to three previously unidentified underground cables, resulting in
damage to the cables and a loss of power to the switchyard rear bus and safeguards bus.
Emergency Diesel Generator (EDG) 1-2 automatically started, as expected, due to the loss of
the safeguards bus, to power in-plant safety-related loads. Subsequently, during



                                              14
troubleshooting activities to identify the cables impacted, a signal transmitting device was
connected to the damaged cables in order to locate the cable electrical flow-path, which
resulted in unexpected cycling of EDG 1-2.

The licensee performed two adverse condition evaluations (ACEs) for this event. In both
ACEs, the licensee identified issues with work instructions or troubleshooting governance and
insufficient field oversight during the work activities that resulted in 1) the damage to three
previously unidentified underground cables during HDD that caused the loss of power to the
rear bus and safeguards bus and the automatic actuation of EDG 1-2, and 2) the cycling of
EDG 1-2 due to a signal transmitting device that was introduced into the plant control circuitry
during unauthorized troubleshooting of the damaged cables.

The inspectors independently reviewed the causal analysis process and corrective actions
(CAs) taken. During review of corrective actions that had a status as complete, the inspectors
questioned if the information in the completed CAs met the requirements of PI-001,
Section 7.5.5.f.8, which discussed that ensuring electronic copies of any documents needed to
understand, clarify, or validate completion of CA response is either attached to the response or
verified to exist in a quality-controlled records location for future retrieval. Specifically, the
inspectors questioned if information attached to the responses of CAs 26011187-06 and
26011187-13 met the requirements of PI-001. After discussion with Performance Improvement
personnel, the inspectors understood that the licensee determined the requirements of CA
closure were not met and took action to address the identified gap. However, the required
closure reviews of the completed CAs had not yet been completed and would likely have
identified the issues. The licensee recently identified issues in timely closure reviews of CRs
during a self-assessment and have planned corrective actions to address these issues.
Associated identified performance deficiencies were documented under IP 71152A.

Performance Assessment:

The inspectors determined this violation was associated with a minor performance deficiency.
Specifically, the licensee failed to submit an LER within 60 days after the discovery of a
reportable event, contrary to licensee procedure RA-108, and 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(A).
Enforcement:

The ROP’s significance determination process does not specifically consider the regulatory
process impact in its assessment of licensee performance. Therefore, it is necessary to
address this violation which impedes the NRC’s ability to regulate using traditional
enforcement to adequately deter non-compliance.

example 6.9.d.9 of the NRC Enforcement Policy because the licensee failed to submit an LER
within the required timeframe in accordance with 10 CFR 50.73.

any event of the type described in this paragraph within 60 days after the discovery of the
event.

condition that resulted in manual or automatic initiation of any of the systems listed in
paragraph (a)(2)(iv)(B).

Title 10 CFR 50.73(a)(2)(iv)(B)(8) requires, in part, that the systems to which the requirements
of paragraph (a)(2)(iv)(A) of this section apply are emergency ac electrical power systems,
including emergency diesel generators (EDGs).

event or condition that resulted in the automatic initiation of EDG 1-2. Specifically, on
February 5, 2026, during directional drilling activities, underground control cables were
severed and stretched which resulted in the loss of power supplied from the rear and
safeguards buses and prompting a start of EDG 1-2. This event represented a condition that
was reportable under 10 CFR 50.73.

Section 2.3.2 of the Enforcement Policy.
