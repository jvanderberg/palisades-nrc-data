Dear Fadi Diya:

On July 20, 2026, the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) commenced an inspection at
Palisades Nuclear Plant (Palisades) using Inspection Procedure 93100, “Safety-Conscious
Work Environment Issue of Concern Follow-up.” The NRC conducted this inspection in
response to information obtained through our baseline inspection program and other avenues
indicating possible concerns with the safety-conscious work environment (SCWE) at Palisades.
The inspection team reviewed selected documents and records, observed activities, and
interviewed personnel. On September 3, 2026, the NRC completed the inspection and
discussed the results with you and members of your staff. The results of this inspection are
documented in the enclosed report.

This inspection did not identify any violations of NRC requirements or other significant plant
safety issues. Our inspection team also determined that Palisades maintained a SCWE where
most personnel felt free to raise nuclear safety concerns without fear of retaliation. However, the
NRC concluded that a Chilled Work Environment exists in two specific departments: supply
chain and quality assurance. The NRC found indications that employees in these two work
departments are hesitant to raise safety concerns using all available avenues without fear of
retaliation. This letter and its enclosure describe the scope of our concerns and communicate
specific requests for your response from Holtec International (Holtec) and Palisades.

Our reviews also found that information was available in the corrective action plan (CAP), the
employee concerns program (ECP), and from other sources that provided Palisades leadership
with opportunities to identify challenges in certain aspects of the site safety culture and SCWE.
However, the NRC staff determined that actions taken to the point of this inspection had not
been successful in addressing challenges with SCWE in those work departments.

While these concerns must be addressed promptly to prevent further cultural decline within the
affected departments, the NRC recognizes that, although supply chain and quality assurance
functions are vital to safe plant operations, there are existing mitigating measures in place that
provide defense-in-depth and ensure plant safety is not compromised. These measures include
independent verification of work activities, supervisory oversight, and collaboration across
departments to ensure diverse viewpoints are considered. Furthermore, the NRC found that
universally, Palisades plant personnel are committed to maintaining safe operations and that
individuals adhere to established procedures and prescribed work processes and understand
their responsibility to pause, seek clarification, and ask questions when faced with uncertainty.
Given these mitigating factors, the NRC maintains confidence that the planned restart of the
Palisades facility will not be adversely affected by the isolated SCWE issues identified during
this inspection.

The NRC’s Policy Statement entitled, “Freedom of Employees in the Nuclear Industry
to Raise Safety Concerns without Fear of Retaliation” (Volume 61 of the Federal Register,
page 24336; May 14, 1996), and the NRC Regulatory Issue Summary (RIS) 05-018,
“Guidance for Establishing and Maintaining a Safety-Conscious Work Environment,” dated
August 25, 2005, (Agencywide Documents Access and Management System (ADAMS)
Accession No. ML052220239) describe the NRC expectations in this area. A SCWE is, in part,
an environment in which employees are encouraged and feel free to raise safety concerns
without fear of retaliation. In contrast, a “chilled work environment” is defined as a work
environment where the willingness of a group of employees to report safety or regulatory
concerns is inhibited.

It is important for Holtec and Palisades to assess the culture for these work departments,
address the causes that created and sustained the chilled work environment within these work
groups, and take steps to ensure that all staff at Holtec and Palisades are willing to openly raise
safety concerns at all levels.

The NRC requests that you notify members of the Palisades workforce of the issuance of this
letter, evaluate the environment for raising concerns, and determine what actions are necessary
to ensure that all employees are free and feel free to raise safety concerns and regulatory
issues without fear of retaliation. This evaluation should be conducted by an independent
organization experienced in performing nuclear SCWE assessments and should ensure that the
number and cross-section of individuals interviewed or surveyed, and the scope of those
interviews and surveys, are sufficient to support meaningful conclusions. Prior to initiating the
evaluation, we request that you develop a plan that outlines how you intend to address the
following: 1) any immediate/short term actions which provide reassurance of acceptable
performance during completion of the evaluation; 2) how the evaluators are independent of the
organizations affected; 3) how you will address potential extent of condition in organizations
outside of supply chain and quality assurance; 4) any associated corrective actions and how
you will measure the effectiveness of any corrective actions; and 5) describe how you will
address effectiveness of your corrective action program and ECP.

The NRC requests a copy of your plan for conducting this evaluation within 30 days of the date
of this letter. Approximately 2 weeks after receipt, the NRC would like to meet with you to ensure
we have a common understanding of the plan.

OTHER ACTIVITIES - TEMPORARY INSTRUCTIONS, INFREQUENT AND ABNORMAL

93100 - Safety-Conscious Work Environment Issue of Concern Follow-Up

NRC Inspectors and qualified safety culture assessors performed an assessment
of the safety-conscious work environment (SCWE) using inspection report IP 93100
“Safety-Conscious Work Environment Issue of Concern Follow-Up.” This assessment was
completed to follow up on issues of concern related to SCWE observed from 2025 to present.
The Palisades SCWE was most recently assessed by the NRC in June 2025 during the Biennial
Problem Identification and Resolution (PI&R) inspection, which was documented in Inspection
Report 05000255/2025009 (ML25203A098). That inspection found that, based upon interviews
with licensee staff and review of documents, Palisades maintained a SCWE where personnel
felt free to raise concerns without fear of retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or discrimination.
While the 2025 inspection generally concluded that Palisades maintained a healthy SCWE, the
inspection team noted that some licensee staff viewed work planning, coordination, and
communication as the biggest challenges impacting the work environment.

Since the previous PI&R inspection, Palisades experienced multiple schedule and work scope
changes, senior manager changes, and communications challenges associated with the restart
of the unit that could have contributed to potential concerns associated with safety culture and
SCWE. In addition, during the first half of 2026, the inspectors noted an increase in the number
of anonymously submitted condition reports in the licensee’s corrective action program (CAP).
Based on these factors, the NRC authorized the performance of IP 93100.

The purpose of the 93100 inspection was to inspect the SCWE at Palisades and determine
if indications of a chilled work environment exist; if employees were reluctant to raise safety
or regulatory issues; and if employees were discouraged from raising safety or regulatory
issues. The inspection consisted primarily of reviewing licensee SCWE assessments, reviewing
SCWE-related issues documented in the licensee’s CAP, and conducting interviews with plant
workers. During this inspection, the team conducted focus groups and interviews with more than
120 individuals from operations, security, maintenance, radiation protection, contract
organizations, supply chain, planning and procurement, chemistry, quality assurance, training,
and engineering. The inspection team also interviewed first-line supervisors and conducted
interviews with senior managers at Palisades and Holtec.

INSPECTION RESULTS

Observation: Review of Palisades Safety Conscious Work Environment                   93100

Overall Assessment of SCWE

Overall, based on interviews conducted during focus groups and individually, the team
determined that Palisades maintained a SCWE where most personnel feel free to raise
nuclear safety concerns without fear of retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or discrimination.
Most staff interviewed stated that they would not hesitate to raise nuclear safety and security
concerns through multiple avenues including supervisors and managers, the employee
concerns program (ECP), and the corrective action program (CAP). The Palisades ECP was
viewed favorably by most employees on-site.

Specifically, most personnel interviewed expressed that safety is the overriding priority for the
Palisades management team and the staff. When faced with schedule pressure, or emergent
work, the staff stated that management conveyed the importance of completing work correctly
and safely, even if it takes extra time. While there is schedule pressure because of the
upcoming restart, the staff perceive that management expects staff to take the time they need
to do work correctly, stop and ask questions when unsure, and put safety first. The inspection
team asked if personnel believed this safety-first philosophy would continue after restart, and
those interviewed said they believed that it would continue.

Willingness and Encouragement to Raise Safety or Regulatory Issues

In a few organizations, some people expressed hesitancy to raise concerns because of a
perception that management emphasizes production and do not want to hear about safety
concerns unless they impact ongoing restart work. There is also hesitancy to raise concerns
about issues that are directed or controlled beyond the site’s immediate control. The
inspection team found that most line staff trust their first level of supervision and usually do
not need to raise concerns to progressive levels of management. However, some personnel
expressed hesitation to elevate concerns beyond their first-level supervisor, noting that
turnover and organizational changes have left them uncertain about who to approach at
higher levels.

The inspection team substantiated the presence of a chilled work environment within the
supply chain and quality assurance departments at Palisades. Through interviews with the
majority of personnel in these organizations, the team consistently heard that employees
were hesitant to raise concerns through various avenues, including, in some cases, bringing
concerns directly to the NRC. This reluctance was driven largely by a perceived fear of
retaliation, including the possibility of termination. The current environment has also
negatively affected, for these two departments, the use of established processes for raising
safety issues, such as the corrective action program (CAP) and the employee concerns
program (ECP). Information obtained during the inspection further indicated that managers at
multiple levels may not always be receptive to employees’ safety concerns, heightening the
NRC’s concern. Employees identified several areas where they felt unable to raise issues or
believed their concerns were not being adequately addressed, including work processes
related to procuring and qualifying equipment and procedures governing work control and
execution. Additionally, concerns were raised about the quality assurance department’s ability
to operate independently.

 Additional Observations

 When asked by the inspection team what was the biggest challenge impacting the work
 environment, most of those interviewed stated that work planning, coordination, and
 communication were still challenged. Schedule planning and adherence have not completely
 transitioned from the project-based system used during decommissioning and early in the
 restart to an operating plant system. This has resulted in unpredictable schedule changes
 and, in some cases, inadequate organizational support for work activities as scheduled.
 This is causing concern and frustration for those directly involved with planning and executing
 work in the field.

 No findings or violations were identified during this inspection.
