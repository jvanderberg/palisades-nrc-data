OTHER ACTIVITIES

71152 - Problem Identification and Resolution

Biennial Team Inspection (IP Section 03.04)

The inspectors performed an assessment of the effectiveness of the licensee’s Problem
Identification and Resolution program, use of operating experience, self-assessments
and audits, and safety-conscious work environment.

INSPECTION RESULTS

  Assessment                                                                           71152
  Corrective Action Program Effectiveness

  The inspectors assessed the effectiveness of the license’s Problem Identification and
  Resolution program in identifying, prioritizing, evaluating, and correcting problems. Based on
  the samples reviewed, the inspectors concluded that the licensee’s implementation of the
  Corrective Action Program (CAP) was generally effective and supported nuclear safety.

  Problem Identification

  Based on the items reviewed, the inspectors concluded that the licensee identifies issues
  at a low threshold and appropriately enters these issues into the CAP. The inspectors
  determined that the licensee usually enters problems into the CAP completely and
  accurately. The licensee reviews CAP data for issue trending. However, due to the lower
  number of issue reports in the CAP, most trends are identified through cognitive trending.

  The inspectors performed a 5-year review of the component cooling water system. As part
  of this review, the inspectors reviewed the system return to service plan, walked down the
  system, and reviewed selective corrective actions and condition evaluation documents.

  Problem Prioritization and Evaluation

  Based on the items reviewed, the inspectors determined that the licensee was generally
  effective at prioritizing and thoroughly evaluating issues commensurate with the safety
  significance of the identified problem. The Issue Report Screening Committee and the
  Management Review Committee meetings were generally thorough and intrusive in reviewing
  issues and prioritizing actions. In addition, the inspectors observed a healthy dialogue
  between the members of these committees. The members were engaged and challenged
  each other when dispositioning issues.




                                                3
The inspectors reviewed the apparent cause evaluation (ACE) associated issue report
PAL-03752, Palisades Steam Generator Tube Inspection and Repair. It was noted that,
contrary to RA-102, Corrective Action Program, section 3.4, the ACE did not include an
extent of condition review. The licensee identified this gap during performance of their
Biennial Problem Identification and Resolution Inspection Self-Assessment, where several
ACEs did not include an extent of condition. The trend was entered into the CAP as
Issue Report (IR) PAL-07331 and the licensee is implementing corrective actions. The
inspectors did not identify any additional concerns in this area.

Corrective Actions

Based on the items reviewed, the inspectors determined that the licensee was generally
effective in corrective action implementation. In general, corrective actions for deficiencies
that were safety significant were implemented in a timely manner.

The inspectors noted that the licensee identified a trend in unsatisfactory closure of corrective
actions. Specifically, the trend identified instances where corrective actions were not
performed as prescribed and objective evidence of closure was not provided. The licensee
performed a performance analysis of the trend and corrective actions are planned.

Assessment                                                                             71152
Use of Operating Experience

The inspectors assessed the effectiveness of the licensee’s process for use of operating
experience. The inspectors noted that the licensee reviewed industry and NRC operating
experience information for applicability to the station. Operating experience lessons learned
were communicated and incorporated into plant operations. However, full implementation of
the licensee’s operating experience program, including the program procedures, is planned
for later in the restart process and the inspectors could not fully assess the implementation of
operating experience as part of this inspection. The NRC resident inspectors will assess full
implementation of the operating experience program as part of their routine CAP reviews.

Assessment                                                                             71152
Self-Assessments and Audits

The inspectors assessed the effectiveness of the licensee’s identification and correction of
problems identified through audits and self-assessments. The inspectors determined that
the licensee’s self-assessments and audits were generally effective. The licensee conducted
self-assessments and quality assurance audits throughout the organization. These
assessments and audits were generally effective in identifying issues and opportunities for
improvement at an appropriate threshold. Deficiencies identified in the licensee’s processes
were addressed through the CAP.

The inspectors noted that a quality assurance audit of the CAP identified the program
elements of identification and evaluation as unsatisfactory. Deficiencies were identified
during the Biennial Problem Identification and Resolution Inspection Self-Assessment and
the quality assurance CAP audit and entered into the CAP process. The inspectors did not
identify any additional concerns in this area.

  Assessment                                                                          71152
  Safety Culture and Safety-Conscious Work Environment

  The inspectors assessed the effectiveness of the station’s programs to establish and maintain
  a safety-conscious work environment. During this assessment, the inspectors conducted
  focus groups and interviews of over 90 individuals in multiple departments including
  Operations, Security, Maintenance, Radiation Protection, Chemistry, and Engineering.
  In addition, the team reviewed documents associated with Safety Culture survey results and
  procedures associated with the Employee Concerns Program (ECP).

  The team observed a Nuclear Safety Culture Monitoring Panel meeting and found it to be
  aligned with the site’s procedure and the NEI 09-07 “Fostering a Healthy Nuclear Safey
  Culture” guidance. The team observed the panel members engaging in robust dialogue and
  self-assessment during the meeting. They asked each other thoughtful, probing questions
  to understand the safety culture related information being discussed.

  Based on interviews, the inspectors determined that the licensee has maintained a
  safety-conscious work environment (SCWE) where personnel feel free to raise safety
  concerns without fear of retaliation, intimidation, harassment, or discrimination. All staff
  interviewed stated that they would not hesitate to raise nuclear safety and security concerns
  through multiple avenues including supervisors and managers, and the CAP. The inspectors
  found that personnel indicated they were aware of the ECP, knew who managed it, and how
  to get in touch with that manager. The inspectors determined that trust seems to permeate
  the work environment from the top down, and that no one had concerns about retaliatory
  behavior for raising issues.
